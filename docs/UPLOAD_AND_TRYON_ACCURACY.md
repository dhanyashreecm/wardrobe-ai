# Clothing upload accuracy: what was wrong, what changed, what was measured

## First, two things this report does not claim

**No photograph was supplied to this work.** The brief referred to "the
generated floor-photo T-shirt image available in this conversation".
No image reached the environment doing the work, and nothing of the
sort was in the repository, so that specific case was not tested. What
replaced it is a 14-case benchmark with exact ground-truth masks,
described below — which is in some ways better (it can be scored
rather than admired) and in one way worse (the images are composites,
not phone photographs).

**No virtual try-on image was generated.** Nothing here measures
try-on realism, because measuring it needs real generations on a free
GPU that is not reachable from this environment. The garment images
that go *into* the model are measurably better, and FASHN VTON v1.5
expects exactly the clean flat-lay-on-white that the pipeline produces,
so better input should mean better output. That is a reasoned
expectation, not a result. Treat any claim that "try-on accuracy
improved" as unproven until real images are compared side by side.

## What was actually wrong

The upload pipeline was already built for real-world photos — floor,
bed, hanger, poor light — with rembg as the preferred engine and
OpenCV GrabCut as a fallback. Three real defects sat underneath that:

1. **rembg was never installed**, on either laptop. Every upload took
   the fallback path and nobody knew, because nothing reported which
   engine ran or how well it did.
2. **Nothing checked whether a cut-out was any good.** The only test
   was "does the mask cover between 3% and 97% of the frame", which a
   badly wrong mask passes easily. A white T-shirt on white bedding
   came out as an L-shaped scrap of bedsheet — and was stored, and
   then sent to the try-on model as the garment.
3. **The classifier could apply a confidently wrong label.** "Blouse",
   "Saree" and "Lehenga" were not in the weak-class list, so a crop
   top became a Blouse at 0.88 confidence and a saree became a Lehenga
   at 0.80, silently, with no question asked.

A fourth, found while writing the tests: the classifier could offer
**"Petticoat"**, a label that is not a category this app stores. An
item saved with it would have dropped out of every recommendation.

## What changed in segmentation

A mask is now checked before it is trusted, on four independent
signals:

* **share of the frame** — between 3% and 97%, as before;
* **compactness** — one object lying inside the photo, not something
  running off every side;
* **bounding-box fill** — a garment is a solid shape. Every correct
  mask in the benchmark fills 0.69–0.82 of its own bounding box; the
  white-on-white failure filled 0.16 while covering a share that looked
  perfectly reasonable. This is the check that catches it, and the only
  one that does;
* **edge alignment** — how much of the mask's outline sits on a real
  edge in the photo, relative to the image's average edge strength.
  Every correct mask scored 1.5–15. The one badly wrong neural mask
  scored 0.59.

The engine choice follows from that: take rembg's mask when it passes,
fall back to GrabCut when it does not, and keep the photo untouched
when neither is usable — and **say so**, which it previously did not
when segmentation returned nothing at all.

This matters because **both engines fail, in different ways**. rembg
lost three-quarters of a dress against a patterned throw (IoU 0.246)
where GrabCut was fine (0.955). GrabCut destroyed the white-on-white
shirt (0.003) where rembg was fine (0.997). Neither is trustworthy
alone; the checks are what make the pair trustworthy.

## The numbers

14 cases, scored as intersection-over-union against the true garment
shape. Generated by `backend/tests/segmentation_bench/make_bench.py`;
`score.py` in the same folder re-runs any engine.

| | mean IoU | worst case |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** (GrabCut, as shipped) | 0.896 | **0.003** — white shirt on white bedding, stored anyway |
| **After**, rembg not installed | **0.949** | 0.724 |
| **After**, rembg installed | **0.970** | 0.831 |

Per case, before → after (with rembg):

| case | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| white t-shirt on white bedding | 0.003 | **0.997** |
| t-shirt on a patterned throw | 0.811 | 0.999 |
| t-shirt on a tiled floor | 0.978 | 0.999 |
| t-shirt on a bed | 0.992 | 0.998 |
| t-shirt on a wooden floor | 0.992 | 0.999 |
| black t-shirt on a dark floor | 0.977 | 0.996 |
| t-shirt, phone blur | 0.946 | 0.982 |
| t-shirt, dim light | 0.993 | 1.000 |
| t-shirt with a printed logo | 0.992 | 0.885 |
| striped t-shirt on tiles | 0.934 | 0.831 |
| jeans on a tiled floor | 0.990 | 0.954 |
| jeans on a wooden floor | 0.992 | 1.000 |
| floral dress on a bed | 0.993 | 0.999 |
| dress on a patterned throw | 0.955 | 0.943 |

Three cases got slightly **worse** with rembg (logo tee, striped tee,
jeans on tiles — it trims a few pixels where GrabCut was generous).
They are reported here rather than left out. The trade is worth it: the
losses are in the third decimal of a usable cut-out, the gain is a
garment that used to be destroyed.

`backend/tests/segmentation_bench/before_after.png` shows three of
these side by side. It is worth looking at — the white-on-white "before"
is not a slightly rough cut-out, it is an unrecognisable scrap. It also
shows, honestly, that the dress on the patterned throw still carries a
fringe of the throw's pattern around the hem in every version.

## What changed in classification

The model was **not retrained** — that is weeks of work and a different
project. What changed is when it is allowed to decide.

`Blouse`, `Saree` and `Lehenga` joined the weak-class list, so they are
never applied automatically however confident the model sounds, and the
alternatives offered are what the model is actually confused *with*:

| the model says | the user is now asked |
|---|---|
| Leggings & Salwars (bootcut jeans) | Leggings · Salwar · **Jeans** |
| Blouse (a crop top) | Blouse · **Crop Top** · Top |
| Lehenga (a saree) | Lehenga · **Saree** · Skirt |
| Saree (a lehenga) | Saree · **Lehenga** · Dupatta |

Classes the model is genuinely reliable on still go straight through —
a confident Shirt or pair of Jeans is applied without interrupting
anyone. A gate that asks about everything is a gate nobody reads.

The "Petticoat" bug is fixed: a photo the model calls a petticoat now
offers Skirt or Lehenga, both of which exist.

**The cost, stated plainly:** adding a saree to the wardrobe now takes
one extra click. That is the price of never again recording a saree as
a lehenga, which was the complaint.

## Person photos

A dim or out-of-focus person photo now produces a note beside the saved
photo — not a refusal. The photo is still accepted, because ordinary
indoor phone photos are a bit dim and a bit soft and refusing them
would be exactly the "photograph yourself against a white wall" demand
this app exists to avoid. The thresholds are the ones the clothing
upload already used, so the two halves of the app agree about what
"dark" and "blurry" mean.

## Tests

`python -m unittest discover -s backend/tests -t .` — **335 tests**,
passing both with and without rembg installed. New:

* `test_segmentation_quality.py` — 10 tests that score the pipeline
  rather than just running it: a mean-IoU floor of 0.90, a per-case
  floor of 0.55, named floors for the everyday floor-and-bed photos,
  and a specific test that the white-on-white case is either segmented
  properly or declined, never mangled. The thresholds are set for the
  no-rembg case, because that is what a fresh clone has.
* `test_classifier_gating.py` — 11 tests pinning both halves of the
  rule: the four confident mistakes ask, and the reliable classes do
  not. One of them found the Petticoat bug.

Frontend: **61 tests**, `npm run build` compiles with no warnings.

## What you have to do by hand

**Install rembg on both laptops.** This is the single biggest
improvement available and it is one command:

```
pip install rembg onnxruntime
```

The first upload afterwards downloads a ~170 MB model once; after that a
cut-out takes about 1.5 s on a laptop CPU. Nothing breaks without it —
the app is simply 0.949 instead of 0.970, with a rougher worst case.

`BG_REMOVAL_ENGINE` can be set to `auto` (default), `rembg`, `grabcut`
or `none` for comparing engines by hand.

## What is still true, and still a limitation

* The classifier is still an IndoFashion model that has never seen a
  crop top. It will still guess wrongly — it just has to ask now.
* The dress-on-a-patterned-throw case is imperfect in every version.
* The benchmark is composites. A real phone photo has motion blur,
  rolling-shutter skew, mixed colour temperature and a cat in the
  corner. The numbers above are a floor on difficulty, not a ceiling.
* Nothing here has been shown to improve a generated try-on image,
  because no try-on image was generated. The garment going in is
  cleaner and more faithful, which is the input the model wants — but
  that chain ends in an expectation, not a measurement.
